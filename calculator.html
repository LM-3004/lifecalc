How to Use the Budget Calculator

This budget calculator and personal budget calculator helps you understand your finances and determine whether you can afford a purchase. Here's how to use it:

  • Enter your income: Input how much you earn, selecting whether it's weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or annual. The calculator will convert this to a weekly amount for consistent comparison.
  • List your regular expenses: Add your ongoing costs like housing, food, transport, bills, entertainment, debt repayments, and any other regular expenses. You can enter each in whatever frequency makes sense (weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or annual).
  • Add the purchase price: Enter the total cost of the item or experience you want to buy.
  • Set a savings buffer: Choose how much money you'd like to keep in savings after the purchase (the default is $1,000). This represents your financial safety net.
  • Review your results: The calculator will show whether you can afford the purchase while maintaining your chosen savings buffer, and if not, how long it might take to save for it.

How Does the Calculator Work?

Our purchase affordability calculator and monthly budget calculator breaks down your finances into simple steps to help you understand your saving capacity:

  1. Normalize your timeline: All income and expenses are converted to a weekly amount, regardless of how frequently you're paid or pay your bills. This makes everything comparable and easy to understand.
  2. Calculate weekly surplus: The calculator subtracts your total weekly expenses from your weekly income to find out how much money you have available to save each week.
  3. Determine your savings target: Your savings target is calculated by adding the purchase price to your desired minimum savings buffer. This is the total amount you need to have saved before making the purchase.
  4. Compare current savings to target: The calculator compares your current savings with the target amount needed. If you have enough already, you can afford it. If not, it shows how much more you need.
  5. Estimate the timeline: If additional savings are needed, the calculator divides the shortfall by your weekly available savings to estimate how many weeks it would take to reach your goal.

What Should I Include in My Budget?

To get the most accurate estimate from your monthly expenses calculator or personal budget planner, include all regular expenses you actually pay:

  • Housing / rent: Your monthly rent or mortgage payment.
  • Food: Groceries and regular food expenses.
  • Transport: Car payments, fuel, public transport, maintenance, or bike costs.
  • Bills / utilities: Electricity, water, internet, phone, and other utility bills.
  • Entertainment / discretionary: Dining out, hobbies, subscriptions, and entertainment spending.
  • Debt repayments: Credit card payments, personal loans, student loans, or other debt obligations.
  • Other expenses: Insurance, healthcare, childcare, personal care, or any other regular costs.

Be as accurate as possible with your expenses. If you're unsure about a regular expense, review your bank statements from the last few months to find the average amount.

What Does the Result Mean?

Once you click Calculate, you'll see several pieces of information about your purchase affordability:

  • Money remaining after purchase: This shows how much cash you'd have left after making the purchase.
  • Savings buffer status: The calculator indicates whether you'd have at least your chosen minimum savings buffer remaining after the purchase.
  • Amount still needed: If you don't currently have enough to afford the purchase while maintaining your savings buffer, this shows how much more you'd need to save.
  • Estimated weeks to save: Based on your available weekly savings, the calculator estimates how long it would take to reach your goal at your current saving rate.
  • Alternative saving scenarios: The results include estimates for what would happen if you increased your weekly savings by $25, $50, $100, or $200. This helps you see how small changes in your spending can speed up your savings timeline.

Can I Afford This Purchase? How to Make a Budget

The calculator provides an estimate based on the information you enter. It assumes your income and expenses remain consistent and doesn't account for unexpected costs or changes in your financial situation. When using this income and expenses calculator, also consider:

  • Whether your income and expenses are likely to change in the near future
  • Any upcoming expenses you may have forgotten to include
  • The long-term value or necessity of the purchase
  • Your comfort level with the savings buffer you've chosen

Use this calculator as a helpful planning tool, but combine it with your own judgment and financial knowledge when making purchase decisions.

Budget Example

Let's walk through a simple example to show how the budget calculator works:

  • Monthly income: $4,000
  • Housing: $1,500 per month
  • Food: $400 per month
  • Transport: $200 per month
  • Bills: $150 per month
  • Entertainment: $200 per month
  • Other expenses: $150 per month
  • Total monthly expenses: $2,600
  • Monthly surplus: $1,400
  • Current savings: $2,000
  • Purchase price: $1,500
  • Minimum savings buffer: $1,000

The calculation: Your savings target (what you need to have saved) is $2,500 ($1,500 purchase + $1,000 buffer). You currently have $2,000, so you need another $500. With a weekly surplus of about $323 ($1,400 monthly ÷ 4.33 weeks), you could reach your savings goal in approximately 1-2 weeks.

In this example, you could afford the purchase fairly quickly while still maintaining a $1,000 financial safety net.
