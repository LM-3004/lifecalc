How to Use the Car Affordability Calculator

This car loan calculator helps you understand the total cost of buying a car and whether it fits your budget. Here's how to use it step by step:

  • Enter the car price: Input the total cost of the vehicle you're considering.
  • Add your current savings: Enter how much money you currently have available.
  • Set your deposit: Enter how much of your savings you plan to use as an upfront deposit on the car.
  • Choose a loan term: Select the length of your car loan, from 1 to 7 years. A longer term means smaller monthly payments but more total interest.
  • Enter the interest rate: Input the annual interest rate you expect on your car loan.
  • Add your income: Enter your regular income and select the frequency (weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or yearly).
  • List existing expenses: Enter your current monthly expenses and their frequency so the calculator can see what you have left for the car.
  • Add ongoing car costs: Enter your estimated monthly costs for insurance, fuel, maintenance/servicing, and registration or other car-related expenses.
  • Review your results: The calculator will show your estimated loan amount, monthly repayment, total car costs, and money remaining after all expenses.

How Does the Car Affordability Calculator Work?

Understanding how this monthly car cost calculator works helps you see the full picture of car ownership:

  • Calculate the loan amount: The estimated loan is the car price minus your deposit. For example, a $30,000 car with a $5,000 deposit results in a $25,000 loan.
  • Estimate monthly loan repayment: The calculator uses your loan amount, interest rate, and loan term to estimate your monthly payment using a standard loan repayment formula. This accounts for the interest you'll pay over the life of the loan.
  • Include ongoing car costs: Your monthly insurance, fuel, maintenance, and registration costs are added to your loan repayment to show your total monthly car cost.
  • Factor in existing expenses: The calculator converts your existing expenses to a monthly amount (whether you entered them weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or yearly) and subtracts them from your income.
  • Calculate money remaining: Your estimated monthly income minus your existing expenses and total car costs shows how much money you'd have left each month.
  • Show total interest paid: The calculator also displays the total interest you'll pay over the life of the loan, which is the difference between all your repayments and the original loan amount.

Car Affordability Example

Here's an example using this car payment calculator to understand the calculation:

  • Car price: $30,000
  • Current savings: $10,000
  • Deposit: $5,000
  • Loan term: 5 years (60 months)
  • Interest rate: 7%
  • Income: $1,000 per week
  • Existing expenses: $400 per month
  • Insurance: $150 per month
  • Fuel: $200 per month
  • Maintenance: $100 per month
  • Registration/other: $50 per month

The calculation: The estimated loan amount is $25,000 ($30,000 – $5,000 deposit). With a 7% interest rate over 5 years, the estimated monthly loan repayment would be approximately $495.03.

The total monthly car cost would be approximately $995.03 ($495.03 loan + $150 insurance + $200 fuel + $100 maintenance + $50 registration). Your monthly income is approximately $4,333, and your existing expenses are $400, leaving about $3,333. After subtracting the $995.03 car cost, you'd have approximately $2,338 per month remaining.

What Costs Should I Consider When Buying a Car?

Car ownership involves several types of ongoing costs beyond the purchase price and loan repayment. When thinking about how much car you can afford, it's important to budget for:

  • Loan repayments: Your monthly payment for financing the car purchase.
  • Insurance: Mandatory or optional coverage depending on your situation and the loan terms.
  • Fuel: Regular fuel costs, which vary based on fuel prices, how much you drive, and your vehicle's fuel efficiency.
  • Maintenance and servicing: Regular oil changes, tire rotations, inspections, repairs, and other upkeep.
  • Registration: Annual or periodic vehicle registration fees.
  • Other costs: Roadside assistance, tolls, parking, vehicle inspection, or any other car-related expenses.

All these costs add up and affect your monthly budget. Using this car affordability calculator helps you see the complete picture of what a purchase might cost each month.

Important Note

This car payment calculator provides estimates based on the information you enter. The actual costs and figures may vary significantly from these estimates:

  • Loan repayments depend on your actual approved interest rate, which varies by lender, your credit history, and market conditions.
  • Insurance costs vary based on your age, driving history, location, the specific car model, and your coverage choices.
  • Fuel costs fluctuate with fuel prices and depend on how much you drive and your vehicle's fuel efficiency.
  • Maintenance costs vary depending on the car's make and model, age, and condition.
  • Registration and other car-related fees vary by location and circumstance.

Use this calculator as a planning tool to estimate your potential monthly costs and see how much car may fit within your budget. For specific loan quotes and actual costs, consult with lenders and insurance providers.
