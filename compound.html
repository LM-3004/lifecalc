What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is when you earn interest not only on your original investment, but also on the interest that has accumulated over time. This creates a "snowball effect" where your money grows faster and faster.

For example, if you invest $1,000 and earn 10% interest in the first year, you'd have $1,100. In the second year, you'd earn 10% on the full $1,100, not just the original $1,000. This means you earn $110 in year two, resulting in $1,210. The longer you invest, the more powerful this compounding effect becomes.

This is why compound interest is sometimes called "earning interest on your interest" – and it's one of the most powerful tools for building long-term wealth.

Why Does Compounding Frequency Matter?

Compounding frequency is how often interest is added to your account. The main options are daily, monthly, quarterly, and yearly.

The more frequently interest compounds, the faster your money typically grows, because you earn interest on a larger balance sooner. For example:

  • Daily compounding – Interest is calculated and added every day, giving you more opportunities to earn interest on interest.
  • Monthly compounding – Interest is added once per month, which is common for savings accounts.
  • Quarterly compounding – Interest is added four times per year.
  • Yearly compounding – Interest is added once per year, which provides the slowest growth.

This compound interest calculator allows you to easily compare how different compounding frequencies affect your savings growth calculator projections. Try adjusting the compounding frequency to see how it influences your final balance.
