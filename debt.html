A debt payoff calculator estimates how long it may take to repay a debt based on your balance, interest rate, and payment amount. It can also compare your current payment with an extra payment scenario so you can see how much a higher payment might reduce the total interest.

How to Use the Debt Payoff Calculator

This debt repayment calculator is designed to help you estimate your payoff timeline with the details you enter. Follow these steps:

  • Current debt balance – Enter the outstanding amount you still owe.
  • Annual interest rate – Add the interest rate applied to the debt, such as a credit card or personal loan rate.
  • Current payment – Enter your regular payment amount.
  • Payment frequency – Choose how often you make that payment, such as weekly, fortnightly, or monthly.
  • Extra payment – If you can pay more than your regular amount, enter the extra amount here.
  • Extra payment frequency – Select how often that extra payment is made.

Once you submit the form, the calculator compares the current repayment scenario with the extra payment scenario to estimate how long it may take to pay off the debt and how much interest may be payable.

How Does the Debt Payoff Calculator Work?

The debt payoff calculator uses the information you enter to estimate a repayment plan. It converts your regular payment and extra payment into a monthly amount so it can compare the scenarios consistently.

  • Payments converted to a monthly amount – Weekly and fortnightly payments are converted into an equivalent monthly amount so the repayment plan can be estimated on a consistent basis.
  • Interest rate effect – A higher annual interest rate increases the proportion of each payment that goes to interest, which can extend the time needed to pay off the debt.
  • Estimated payoff time – The calculator estimates how many months it would take to eliminate the debt balance with the current payment and with the extra payment scenario.
  • Total payments and interest – The estimates include the total amount paid and the estimated interest charged over the life of the debt.
  • Extra payment comparison – The calculator compares your current payment against a scenario where you add an extra payment, helping show how that additional amount can affect the payoff time and interest paid.

Because loan terms and lender rules vary, these results are estimates rather than guaranteed repayment schedules.

What Does Extra Payment Mean?

The extra payment is an additional amount you choose to add on top of your current payment. In this debt payoff calculator, the extra payment is entered separately so you can compare two repayment scenarios:

  • Your current payment only.
  • Your current payment plus the extra amount you choose.

The calculator then estimates how that extra payment changes the projected debt payoff time and the interest that may be paid. The key point is that a higher repayment amount can reduce how long it takes to pay off debt, but actual results still depend on the terms of the debt and lender calculations.

Important Note

This debt payoff calculator provides estimates based on the information entered. Actual debt repayment results can vary depending on:

  • Interest calculation methods used by the lender.
  • Any fees, charges or penalties charged on the account.
  • Payment timing and when interest is applied.
  • Lender rules, account terms and repayment conditions.
  • Changes in rates or payment amounts over time.

These figures are estimates for planning and comparison purposes only. The debt payoff calculator is a helpful debt interest calculator for understanding potential repayment scenarios, but it is not a guarantee of actual lender outcomes.
